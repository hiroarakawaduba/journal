Disconnect to Reconnect

The uninterrupted, chronological daily logs of a 30-day experiment tracking focus, attention limits, and mindfulness away from digital noise.

Day 1

The Shock of Sudden Silence

Personal Experience: Sat down at my computer to start schoolwork and instinctively went to type in a social site URL. Had to catch myself and close the tab. Felt a strange restlessness without that quick digital break.

Observations & Visuals: Noticed how often classmates at school check their phones during downtime. Since I don't have one, I usually just watch the room, but today it felt even more obvious how disconnected everyone seemed.

Mindfulness Action: Did a 5-minute box breathing exercise (inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s, hold 4s) at my desk to clear that restless feeling.

#Habits #Mindfulness
Day 2

Time Wealth & Relieved Eyes

Personal Experience: Had a lot of extra time at home after finishing homework because I didn't spend an hour browsing feeds on my laptop.

Observations & Visuals: My eyes feel significantly less tired in the evening from the reduced overall screen time.

Mindfulness Action: Mindful eating during lunch. Instead of thinking about what I was missing online, I focused entirely on the environment around me.

Day 3

Out of the Loop, but Grounded

Personal Experience: Missed an inside joke or a trend that my friends were talking about from the night before. Felt a little out of the loop, but the feeling passed quickly.

Observations & Visuals: Looked around the bus today. It looks surreal when almost every single person is looking down at a small screen in their hands.

Practical Research Highlight: Added to Poster 1: Observations show that public spaces are increasingly silent due to mobile device immersion, reducing spontaneous face-to-face interactions.

#Research
Day 4

Sitting with the Quiet

Personal Experience: Evening boredom hit around 8:00 PM when my computer was shut down. Usually, I'd look for entertainment online, but I had to sit with the quiet instead.

Mindfulness Action: 10-minute body scan meditation. Realized how much tension I hold in my shoulders when I'm trying to force myself to relax.

Day 5

Fading Urges and Dopamine Loops

Personal Experience: The automatic urge to open bookmarks or social tabs on my computer is fading. My brain is starting to accept the new boundaries.

Observations & Visuals: Drafted the visual layout for my first project poster, mapping out the connection between constant connectivity and attention spans.

Academic Research Highlight: Added to Poster 1: Research indicates that even without a personal mobile device, heavy desktop media consumption triggers similar dopamine reward loops that disrupt deep focus.

#Poster1 #Research
Day 6

Sustained Hobby Focus

Personal Experience: Spent the afternoon working on hobbies without any digital distractions. My focus felt much more sustained.

Observations & Visuals: Noticed I am finishing my reading assignments faster because I'm not jumping back and forth between tabs.

Day 7

Liberation and the 5 Senses

Personal Experience: One week complete! It feels liberating to have my computer strictly be a tool for work rather than a destination for distraction.

Mindfulness Action: 5-senses outdoor walk (focusing on 5 things I can see, 4 I can touch, 3 I can hear, 2 I can smell, 1 I can taste) to ground myself in the physical world.

Day 8

Melatonin Balance & Sleep Depth

Personal Experience: My sleep quality has noticeably improved. Getting away from all screens an hour before bed has made a massive difference.

Academic Research Highlight: Added to Poster 2: Evening screen use suppresses melatonin. Completely cutting out nighttime browsing directly improves the depth of REM sleep cycles.

#Poster2 #Research
Day 9

Clearing the Brain Fog

Personal Experience: Felt a high level of mental clarity during a difficult school day. Brain fog from constant information consumption is gone.

Mindfulness Action: Focused on mindful breathing for 3 minutes right before a quiz to lower my heart rate and settle my thoughts.

Day 10

The Weekend Wall

Personal Experience: Hit a wall today. Felt a bit isolated from what people were doing online over the weekend.

Observations & Visuals: Sketched a brain diagram for my second poster, focusing on the prefrontal cortex and its role in attention management.

Day 11

The Default Mode Network

Personal Experience: Pushed past yesterday's slump. Realized that quiet time isn't "wasted" time; it's empty space that gives my mind room to breathe.

Practical Research Highlight: Allowing the brain to experience boredom activates the "default mode network," which is essential for creative problem-solving and memory consolidation.

#Research
Day 12

Direct Conversations

Personal Experience: Had to actively seek out school updates by asking people directly rather than looking for posted announcements online. It actually led to a few good conversations.

Mindfulness Action: Simple awareness meditation—observing thoughts pass by like clouds without judging or reacting to them.

Day 13

Poster 1 Hits the Hallway

Personal Experience: Finished and put up my first poster in the hallway today to share my initial observations on attention spans.

Observations & Visuals: Watched a few students stop to read the poster while simultaneously looking up from their phones. The contrast was fascinating.

Day 14

Reclaiming Patience

Personal Experience: Two weeks in. The craving for instant, bite-sized entertainment on the computer has dropped to zero. I feel much more patient.
